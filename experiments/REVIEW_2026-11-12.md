# Review 2026-11-12/13: why the closed loop fails where the open-loop fit is good

Scope: the brief of 2026-11-12 (bugs and method errors behind the open-loop / closed-loop gap, literature, a ranked
plan, repo cleanup). Branch `review/2026-11-12`, nothing pushed. Nothing in `data/` or `outputs/` was modified or
deleted; everything new there starts with `review_`, plus the leftovers listed in [section 6](#6-leftovers).

Subjects: the expert-data camera policy `outputs/20261111-1316_dp_240_pre_expert_v2` (200 scripted takes, level 1
2%) and, less completely, the human-data policy `outputs/20261106-2147_dp_240_pre_two_bricks_v4` (level 1 16%).

## 1. Summary

1. **The expert-data policy fails on its own training scenes**, within 1–2 s of taking over from a demo state. The
   failure does not need states far from the data, so more demos of the same kind will not fix it.
2. **The loop is unstable through the joint velocity in `observation.state`.** In the demos the target leads the
   measured joints by 0.12 s of motion (`target − q ≈ 0.12 s × q̇`, R² 0.99), so the policy can read its action off
   the velocity. In closed loop that is a feedback path: a slowed arm is told to keep going slowly. Drift grows with
   how often the policy re-plans, and replacing only the velocity input by the demo's removes most of it.
3. **"1.4 mm open-loop fit" was not evidence of precision.** A predictor that knows nothing of the task ("keep
   coasting") scores 0.1–0.6 mm on the same frames.
4. **The decisive test is only half decided.** Two camera-free policies (true brick poses, with and without
   velocity, 20k steps) both score 0/50 on level 1. Without velocity the blue brick is carried to within 10 mm of
   its goal in 16/50 rollouts against 2/50, and the drift at one step per chunk is 3–5× smaller. Both models are
   under-trained (3–7 mm open loop), so removing velocity is supported as necessary, not shown to be sufficient.
5. **The camera policy without velocity has not been trained yet.** The dataset is exported
   (`data/lerobot/expert_v2_novel`); the run is the first item of the plan.
6. The safety filter, observation/action alignment, labels, crop and image normalization were checked and are
   correct.

## 2. Findings

Scripts are in [obs_ablation/diagnostics/](obs_ablation/diagnostics/) (`e2`…`e8`) and `scripts/`. "Drift" is the
distance of the moving arm's TCP from the demo at the same step, median over 6 training takes, started 5 s and
3.5 s before level 1 clicks.

### Verified (measured)

**V1. Failure on training scenes** (`e2_restart.py`). Closed loop from recorded states of 6 training takes: level 1
clicked 0/6 from t = 0, 1/6 from 8 s before the click, 0/6 (6 s), 2/6 (4 s), 1/6 (3 s), 1/6 (2 s), 5/6 (1 s). Blue
ends 6–20 mm off, on the stud tops.

**V2. The safety filter is not the cause** (`e3_drift.py`, `scripts/drift_test.py` row "replay"). The demo's
targets replayed through it stay at 0.0 mm; policy drift is the same with the filter off.

**V3. Drift grows with re-planning frequency** (expert-data camera policy, start 5 s before the click):

| steps per chunk | after 16 | 32 | 48 | 64 | 96 steps |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 64 (open loop) | 0.9 | 0.9 | 1.1 | 1.3 | 2.9 mm |
| 32 | 0.9 | 0.9 | 1.3 | 1.3 | 3.9 |
| 16 (trained) | 0.7 | 1.4 | 2.7 | 3.5 | 6.2 |
| 8 | 1.7 | 2.8 | 4.4 | 5.6 | 11.5 |
| 1 | 4.8 | 9.8 | 13.2 | 19.7 | 29.6 |

From 3.5 s before the click, 1 step per chunk reaches 123 mm after 64 steps. The existing evaluations agree: 8
steps per chunk scored below 16 in all four runs evaluated both ways (16→8%, 14→8%, 2→0%, state oracle 22→2%).
`scripts/train_and_eval.sh` therefore now runs its second evaluation with chunks twice as long, not half as long.

**V4. Velocity is the main feedback path** (`e4_channels.py`; one observation channel replaced by the demo's value
at each step). Drift after 64 steps at 1 step per chunk, starts 5 s / 3.5 s:

| forced channel | drift, mm |
|---|---|
| none | 21.6 / 128.3 |
| joint velocity | 7.6 / 8.3 |
| joint position | 6.4 / 11.6 |
| images | 3.6 / 62.3 |
| hand wrench | 159.5 / 120.7 |
| position + velocity + wrench | 2.9 / 2.5 |

At the trained 16 steps per chunk, after 128 steps: none 30.6 / 7.4; velocity forced 3.1 / 3.1; wrench 30.2 / 6.7;
images 23.5 / 6.1.

**V5. The shortcut is in the data** (`e5_shortcut.py`). `target − q ≈ 0.12 s × q̇` explains 99.2% of the variance of
the target's lead in expert_v2, 96% in the retimed human takes and 95.5% in the raw human takes. The expert's
targets follow a time programme; the measured velocity is their consequence, not their cause.

**V6. The evaluation rollouts show the same thing** (`e7_transit.py`). Final transit of the right arm: peak hand
speed 0.229 m/s in the demos, 0.195 (16 per chunk) and 0.186 (8 per chunk) in rollouts. Blue is 10.7 mm (xy) from
its goal when the descent starts in the demos, 29.6 and 41.7 mm in the rollouts. Slower transit goes with a larger
shortfall (r = −0.62). This is the "lands 2–7 cm short on the approach side" of the report.

**V7. The open-loop number flatters** (`e6_baselines.py`; now printed by `scripts/fit_error.py` as `coast` and
`repeat`). On the place window "keep coasting" (`q + 0.12 q̇`, continued) scores 0.1 / 0.4 / 0.6 mm (first step /
mean / last of 16); the model 2.0 / 1.6 / 1.6 mm on the same frames. The model beats coasting only outside the
slow phases (other: 2.7 vs 5.9 mm mean; grasp: 4.2 vs 7.0). Each fresh chunk starts 2–3 mm off the demo's next
target (human-data model: 4–7 mm). That first-step error is what V4's loop feeds on.

**V8. State oracle with and without velocity** (`review_oracle2_vel`, `review_oracle2_novel`; preset `dp_state1`,
20k steps = 7.5 epochs; evaluated with `e8_eval_pinned.py`, 50 seeds):

| | with velocity | without velocity | camera policy (80k, with velocity) | demos |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| base in area | 98% | 100% | 76% | 100% |
| level 1 | 0% | 0% | 2% | 100% |
| blue carried to within 30 mm of red | 18/50 | 33/50 | 15/50 | 50/50 |
| within 10 mm | 2/50 | 16/50 | 5/50 | 50/50 |
| median closest approach while carried | 54 mm | 17 mm | 38 mm | 8 mm |
| drift after 64 steps, 1 step per chunk | 108 / 131 mm | 35 / 27 mm | 21.6 / 128.3 mm | |
| drift after 64 steps, 16 per chunk (trained) | 33 / 19 mm | 14 / 24 mm | 3.5 mm (5 s start) | |
| open loop (all channels forced), 64 steps | 3.6 / 3.0 mm | 7.0 / 5.7 mm | 2.9 / 2.5 mm | |

Reading: removing velocity brings the brick much closer (16 vs 2 of 50 within 10 mm) and cuts the drift at high
re-planning frequency by 3–5×, but neither oracle clicks a brick. Their open-loop error is 3–7 mm, above the ~3 mm
the studs catch, so they could not have clicked: 7.5 epochs is too little. The test supports "velocity must go",
not "velocity is the whole problem".

**V9. Min-max normalization of an input that never varies breaks the first chunk** (found on my own preset).
`dp_state1` min-max normalizes `observation.environment_state`. Bricks 3 and 4 are never touched in the expert
takes, so their z has range 0. While the bricks settle in the first steps of an eval episode (the trimmed takes
never contain those steps) the normalized z is 11 and 23 instead of −1. Every rollout then pushed the base out,
with safety corrections of 1.5–2 rad. `e8_eval_pinned.py` pins the 6 such inputs to their training value; base in
area went from 0% to 98–100% with no retraining. The numbers in V8 are with the pin.

**V10. LeRobot leaves `observation.environment_state` unnormalized** by default (installed code:
`normalize_processor.py` uses `norm_map.get(feature_type, IDENTITY)`, and the diffusion default mapping has no
`ENV` entry). This applies to the earlier `dp_state` oracle runs. Effect on their results: not measured.

**V11. Checked and correct.** Observation t ↔ action t in export, rollout and LeRobot's `action_delta_indices`;
`executed_targets` (the ctrl of the next state); gripper labels; centre crop at inference; ImageNet statistics;
`drop_n_last_frames`. Normalization resolution: 1 mm at the TCP is 0.001–0.002 of the [−1, 1] action range.

### Likely (evidence, not a controlled test)

**L1. The human-data policy's first problem is the fit, not the loop** (`e4_v4_nas16.log`). Its open-loop error on
training takes is 3–6 mm at every step; closed loop is only moderately worse (4–10 mm over 128 steps); forcing
velocity does not help in that window. The chunk-length drift sweep for this policy was not run.

**L2. Under-training.** 80k steps × batch 32 is 62 epochs of two_bricks_v4 (41k frames) and 15 epochs of expert_v2
(169k frames). Diffusion Policy trains image policies for 3000 epochs, JUICER up to 400 at batch 256. Whether the
open-loop error still falls between checkpoints was not measured (plan item 3 does).

**L3. Velocity also hurts the human-data policies.** The shortcut is nearly as strong there (R² 0.96), and 8 steps
per chunk scored below 16 in their evaluations too. L1 says it is not their largest error today.

### Speculative

**S1. Hand-over stalls with one observation frame.** In 2–3 of 50 oracle rollouts the left gripper never releases
the base and the right arm waits with the blue brick for the rest of the episode. With `n_obs_steps` 1 and no
velocity the state during a wait carries no clock. Not seen in the camera baseline (0/50).

**S2. 50 Hz targets with a 64-step horizon** look ahead only 1.28 s and give 3–6 re-plans per second. Diffusion
Policy and ResiP run at 10 Hz and interpolate. Given V3 a lower policy rate should help; not tested.

**S3. The scripted expert as a teacher.** Its targets are a time programme, so it shows no reaction to an error.
A DAgger round with it only helps if the labels come from a planner that reacts to the current state.

| Candidate | Result |

## 4. Ranked plan

Costs on this laptop (RTX 4050 6 GB, 14 GB RAM): one camera run of 80k steps is 9.5 h; a 50-rollout camera
evaluation 10 min; a state-oracle run of 20k steps 45 min alone.

| # | Step | Fixes (evidence) | Expected gain | Cost | Cheapest confirming test | Stop when |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Camera policy without joint velocity: `expert_v2_novel`, `dp_240_pre` | V3–V6, V8 | drift at 16 per chunk from ~30 mm to the open-loop 3 mm; level 1 from 2% to an unknown level (V8: closer, not yet clicking) | 1 GPU-night, no human time; export is done | pause at checkpoint 030000 (3.5 h): `drift_test.py` (10 min) and 50 rollouts against the baseline's 030000 (measured: base in area 22%, level 1 0%, blue within 30 mm 1/50, within 10 mm 0/50, median closest 233 mm; `e9_approach.py`) | at 030000 the drift at 1 step per chunk is not below half the baseline's and "within 10 mm" is not above it |
| 2 | Longer chunks on the existing models (`--n-action-steps 32`, `64`) | V3 | a few points of level 1, free | 15 min per evaluation of 20 rollouts | is the test | level 1 at 32 is not above 16 on the same 20 seeds |
| 3 | Is the fit still improving? `fit_error.py` on checkpoints 020000…080000 of both camera runs | L1, L2 | decides whether to train longer | 1 h, no training | is the test | error flat from 060000 to 080000: more steps will not help; otherwise resume to 160k (1 night) |
| 4 | Corrective data around the place: expert takes started from perturbed states near the tower, labels from a planner that re-plans from the current state | V1, S3; JUICER | JUICER: +14–15 points | 0.5 day of code, 1 night | 20 such takes mixed in at 030000, drift from perturbed starts | drift from perturbed starts does not fall |
| 5 | Policy at 10–12.5 Hz, targets interpolated to 50 Hz | S2 | fewer re-plans per second, 5 s of lookahead | export change + 1 night | state oracle without velocity at 12.5 Hz (45 min) | oracle "within 10 mm" not above 16/50 |
| 6 | Same recipe on the human takes (`two_bricks_v4` without velocity, longer training) | L1–L3 | unknown | 1–2 nights | after 1 and 3 | 1 fails |
| 7 | Scripted or compliant press for the last centimetre (hybrid) | plain BC ceiling on assembly (JUICER, ResiP) | removes the 1–3 mm requirement from the policy | 1–2 days | replay rollouts that ended within 5 mm with the press primitive | fewer than half of them click |
| 8 | Residual RL (ResiP) | same | 90%+ in the paper | 1024 parallel environments: not on this machine | none here | not started |

Order: 2 and 3 are an hour together and need no training; do them before or while 1 runs (not at the same time as
a camera training: the GPU holds one camera job). 4 only after 1, because corrective data does not remove a
feedback path inside the observation. Every step keeps the policy inputs to what the real FR3 has: cameras, joint
positions, gripper width (and the hand wrench, which V4 shows is not used).
