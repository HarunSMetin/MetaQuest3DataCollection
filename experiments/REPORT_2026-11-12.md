# QuestCollect: report on all learning experiments (2026-10-27 to 2026-11-12)

This report is self-contained. It assumes no knowledge of the project. It covers:
- the task and the simulation;
- the data pipeline and the policy setup;
- every experiment, with its result and diagnostics;
- what we currently believe, the open questions, and the constraints a proposal must respect.

Sections 1–4 are facts and measurements. Section 5 is our interpretation so far. Feel free to disagree with it.

---

## 1. Project and task

**Goal:** imitation learning for **bimanual manipulation with two Franka Panda arms**. Simulation first (MuJoCo), real
FR3 robots later. Demos are recorded by teleoperation from a Meta Quest 3 headset (WebXR page): controller or hand
poses → IK → simulated arms.

**Task (`build_tower`):** stack 4 DUPLO bricks into a fixed tower inside a taped build area (14 × 14 cm) on the
table, centred at (0.45, 0). The bricks, in order:

| level | brick | size (studs) | mass |
|---|---|---|---|
| base | red 2×4 | 4×2×1 | 12.2 g |
| 1 | blue 2×3 | 3×2×1 | 9.6 g |
| 2 | yellow 2×2 | 2×2×1 | 6.8 g |
| 3 | green 1×2×2 | 2×1×2 | 8.0 g |

- **Goal poses:** each brick's goal is fixed relative to the brick below: blue at −0.5 stud along red's x, same yaw;
  and so on.
- **Spawn regions:** each brick spawns in its own fixed region, ±4 cm and ±15° jitter. Red and yellow spawn on the
  left (+y), so the left arm handles them. Blue and green spawn on the right (−y), for the right arm.
- **Two arms are required:** each arm has to fetch its own bricks and hand nothing over.
- **The current sub-task:** since 2026-11-07 we only work on **"two bricks"**: red base in the build area, blue clicked
  onto it (= level 1). The full tower proved too hard. The scene still spawns all 4 bricks; episodes are just cut 1 s
  after level 1 clicks (`curate.until_level: 1`). The metric is **`level_1`** (success = level 1 clicked).

### Physics that matters for precision

**Studs and the click:**
- Bricks have real collision studs: Ø 9.36 mm, 4.5 mm high, a 1.5 mm 45° chamfer on top, 0.3 mm play per side.
- Each brick has a hidden solid core in its hollow underside that only robot geoms hit.
- **The click** (`_update_clutch`) is a weld that switches on when all of these hold:
  - the pair touches;
  - the upper brick sits on the lower one's stud grid: within **1 mm in xy**, 1.5 mm in z, 4° in yaw;
  - the downward contact force exceeds **6 N**.
- The weld switches off above 10 N of pull.

**How far off can a brick land and still seat?**
- Measured: a free brick pressed with 10 N still seats when it lands up to **~3 mm** off. It tilts and slides on the
  chamfers.
- With a 3.5 mm chamfer it would be ~5 mm. The sim was not changed.
- Put differently: **xy placement must be within ~3 mm, and the brick must then be pressed down.** A brick that lands
  further off rests on the stud tops (+4.5 mm high) and does not click.

**Gripper:** a tendon servo with 40 N squeeze. Its target is rate-limited to 50 mm/s per finger, like the real
Franka Hand. Elliptic friction cones.

**Control:** 50 Hz. The action is joint position targets for 7+7 joints plus 2 grippers.

**Safety filter:** an IK/QP that keeps the arms 2 cm apart, links 1 cm and fingertips 5 mm off the table. It sits
on both the teleop path and the policy path.

**Hardware:** laptop with an RTX 4050 (6 GB VRAM), 14 GB RAM, 16 cores. This limits model size and batch size.

## 2. Data and learning pipeline

1. **Record** (HDF5): the full MuJoCo state at every step (`sim/state`), the actions, raw XR poses, contacts and
   progress. No images are recorded; they are re-rendered from the state at export.
2. **Audit / curate**: `qc audit` keeps or excludes episodes and cuts segments (e.g. `until_level`).
3. **Export to LeRobot 0.4.4** (`qc export`): cameras re-rendered at 240×320, matte (no shadows or specular).
4. **Train** with `lerobot-train` (subprocess).
5. **Evaluate in sim** (`qc eval`):
   - 50 rollouts on fixed seeds from a separate seed stream;
   - same scene hash, safety filter on, DART noise off;
   - horizon = longest training segment plus a margin (28 s or 35 s, see the tables).
6. **Check the pipeline** before training (`scripts/check_pipeline.py`, 17 checks):
   - configs are identical between data and eval;
   - recorded actions replay bit-exactly;
   - eval layouts lie inside the training range;
   - dataset state/action equals the source;
   - dataset images equal the live render (≤ 2.2/255).
   Every dataset since two_bricks passed all 17. **Data/eval mismatch has been ruled out.**

**Policy input** (`FeatureSpec`):
- Cameras (current): `cam_low` (side view of the build area) + left and right wrist cameras, 240×320 each.
  Earlier runs used `cam_high` (overhead) + wrists.
- `observation.state`: 16 joint positions (7+grip per arm) + 14 joint velocities + 12 values of hand force/torque
  (log-compressed; a real Franka estimates the same as `O_F_ext_hat_K`). 42 values in total.

**Policy output:** absolute joint position targets, 16-D, ALOHA/ACT layout `[7 left, grip, 7 right, grip]`. The
labels are the **executed** targets (`action_label: executed`). With DART noise these are the noisy targets actually
sent, not the operator's intended ones, because only the executed ones reproduce the physics exactly.

**Main policy: Diffusion Policy** (LeRobot implementation, preset `dp_240_pre`):

| setting | value |
|---|---|
| denoiser | CNN U-Net, down_dims 256/512/1024 |
| image encoder | ResNet18 per camera, ImageNet pretrained, BatchNorm kept, spatial softmax |
| observation steps | n_obs_steps 1 |
| action horizon | 64 steps (1.28 s) |
| executed per chunk | n_action_steps 16 (0.32 s) |
| noise scheduler | DDIM, 100 train / 10 inference steps |
| crop | random crop 0.9 |
| batch / steps | batch 32, 80k steps |
| training time | about 9.5 h on the RTX 4050 |

- Normalization: LeRobot defaults (min-max to [−1, 1] on state and action).
- There is no EMA in LeRobot. We wrote a wrapper (`dp_240_pre_ema`), but no full run has used it yet.
- `dp_240` is the same model, but its encoder is trained from scratch with GroupNorm.
- ACT (`act_240`, chunk 100, batch 16, 100k steps) was used early on.

## 3. Chronology of experiments

All success numbers come from 50 sim rollouts unless stated otherwise. "Level 1" = blue clicked onto red. "Base in
area" = red inside the build area.

### 3.1 tower_v1: ACT on 87 teleop demos (2026-10-27): **0/50**

**Data:** the first human dataset, 4 bricks, wide spawn spread, messy multi-attempt takes.

**Result and diagnosis:**
- 0/50; the policy misses bricks by 3–6 cm.
- The pipeline itself was verified: video frame equals the live render.
- Held-out open-loop error was ~3.6× the training error and stayed flat after 40k steps. In other words, no
  generalization.

**Response:**
- The data was archived.
- The new setup used fixed per-brick spawn regions, matte cameras and a **grasp assist**: during recording it centred
  the grasp on the brick to ~1 mm.
- Supervisor advice: clean single takes, try Diffusion, add joint velocity.

### 3.2 tower_v2: generated and retimed data, ACT (2026-11-02): **0/50**

**Data:**
- 11 new human demos.
- 120 MimicGen-style generated takes, made by transforming each demo's end-effector path to new brick layouts. Each
  take is replayed through IK and physics and kept only on success.
- Then "retimed" so that both arms work at the same time: a move scheduler with arm/camera clearance checks and one
  arm at a time in the tower zone.
- 108 takes, 62.5 min in total. Grasp assist on.

**Result:** ACT `act_240`: 0/50 with chunks as trained, 0/50 with temporal ensembling. Base in area 80%.

| where the blue brick ended | chunks | ensemble |
|---|---|---|
| on the stud tops, not seated (median 6–8 mm off in xy) | 17 | 20 |
| near the base, not on it | 13 | 12 |
| never reached the base (missed grasp, dropped, flung) | 20 | 18 |

- The training demos placed within 0.0–0.3 mm. Nothing in the data lifts a brick off the stud tops and retries.
- ACT also failed when started from the exact first frame of a training episode (2/2 tried).

**Chamfer test:** with a 3.5 mm chamfer, ACT seated blue perfectly in 4 of 20 rollouts but **never clicked**. The
press force was ~0.1 N; 6 N is needed, and the demos press with ~86 N. The policy opened the gripper 1–4 cm above
the base and dropped the brick. It also grasped 19 mm off centre, against ~1.5 mm in the demos thanks to the assist.

**Conclusions at the time:**
- The policy skips the "press until click" phase.
- It grasps far off centre.

**Response:** hand force/torque was added to the state, so the click becomes a force cue.

### 3.3 Diffusion on tower_v2 (`dp_240`, from-scratch encoder; 2026-11-02): **0/50**

- Base in area 48%, level 1 4%.
- Blue never lifted in 29/50: grasping is worse than ACT's.

### 3.4 State oracle: brick poses instead of cameras (2026-11-03)

**Question:** is perception or the action/data side the bottleneck?

**Setup:** `dp_state` (no cameras). The true poses of all 4 bricks go into the state. 60k steps. Data: tower_v2.

| action space | base in area | level 1 | blue on stud tops | blue never lifted |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| joint | 90% | **22%** | 18/50, median **7.3 mm** off | 12/50 |
| end-effector (position + 6D rotation, IK at run time) | 26/26 | one full tower (the only success ever) | 9/25, median **7.9 mm** off | 8/25 |

Further measurements on the end-effector oracle:
- It grasps blue 10 mm off centre; the demos with assist are 0.9 mm.
- On its own training frames, a predicted chunk just before a grasp is 2.1 mm off the demo in xy (p90 3.5 mm) and
  4.5 mm in z.
- More denoising steps (DDIM 30/100, DDPM 100) did not help.
- With the grasp assist applied at eval, grasps were 1.2 mm off, yet blue still landed 6.7 mm off.

**Reading:**
- Perception is part of the problem: with cameras level 1 was 0–4%.
- But even exact state input places about 7 mm off. The action and data side is the larger part.
- End-effector actions are no better than joint actions.
- **Unchecked suspect:** LeRobot does not normalize `observation.environment_state`, so a 1 mm shift is a 0.001
  change of a raw input.

### 3.5 Project decision (2026-11-03): no assists, realistic data

After every policy scored ~0%, the user decided:
- **The grasp assist is removed.** It made every demo grasp perfectly centred, so the data never showed off-centre
  grasps or their correction.
- **The safety filter now also runs on teleop** (only collision prevention).
- **IK smoothness:** the arm bases moved back 5 cm and the IK got a damping task.
- **DART** (correlated Ornstein–Uhlenbeck drift, 0.003 rad ≈ 3.5 mm at the TCP) on the executed operator targets.
- Actions are labelled with the executed targets.
- **Human demos only**, no generated data. This preference was relaxed later in the two-brick work: retimed and
  scripted data were used as experiments.

### 3.6 tower_v3: Diffusion on 71 unassisted human demos (2026-11-06): **0/20 (stopped), level 1 0%**

**Data:**
- 73 recorded, 71 kept; 69.5 min.
- Grasps are now realistically off centre: median 4.6 mm, p90 11.5 mm.
- 21 of 291 brick lifts were regrasps or retries.
- The upward hand force around a click is a median 48 N.
- DART drift near clicks is 2.2 mm std, and the operator corrects only about ⅓ of it. That is why the labels are the
  executed targets.

**Open-loop replay** from `sim/state[0]`, episodes 5, 30, 60 (levels reached):

| replayed | levels |
|---|---|
| recorded joint targets | 1, 1, 2 |
| executed joint targets | 3, 3, 3 (bit-exact) |
| recorded TCP targets → IK | 1, 2, 1 |
| executed TCP targets → IK | 3, 2, 2 |

**Result:** `dp_240` (from-scratch encoder, `cam_high` + wrists): 0/20, eval stopped early. Base in area ~60%,
level 1 0%.
- Chunks were already 3–15 mm off the training frames.
- Grasps in eval were 10–39 mm off centre.

### 3.7 two_bricks_v4: the sub-task, retimed human takes, pretrained encoder (2026-11-07): **level 1 16%** (best camera result)

**Changes:**
- Sub-task cut at level 1.
- Retimed versions of the human tower_v3 takes, so both arms work at once and the waiting arm stages just outside the
  zone. 50 takes, 13.7 min. Retimed files are re-simulated without DART, so recorded = executed.
- Cameras `cam_low` + wrists instead of `cam_high`.
- `dp_240_pre` (ImageNet ResNet18).

**Result:** run `20261106-2147_dp_240_pre_two_bricks_v4`.

| eval | base in area | level 1 |
|---|---|---|
| final 80k, 16 steps per chunk | 68% | **16%** (8/50) |
| final 80k, 8 steps per chunk | 60% | 8% |
| 20k checkpoint | 22% | 0% |
| 40k checkpoint | 52% | 0% |
| 60k checkpoint | 84% | 14% |
| 60k + 80k weights averaged (an EMA stand-in) | 82% | 14% |
| 60k checkpoint, 35 s horizon | 82% | 14% |

The model improves up to 60k and then levels off. At n = 50 a difference of ±5% is noise.

**Failure breakdown** (final model, 16 steps per chunk; 42 failures):
- **Blue never lifted: 13.**
- **Blue lifted but never put on red: 22.** Mostly held 2–4 cm above or beside the tower for 10–24 s until the time
  ran out, while the other arm was ~20 cm away. The demos hover there for at most 2–4 s.
- **Blue on red but not clicked: 7.** 3–22 mm off. It does press (90–125 N), so the force cue was learned.

**Grasp quality is the root:** TCP to brick centre at lift-off:

| | red | blue |
|---|---|---|
| policy | 11.7 mm | 19.8 mm |
| demos | 3.8 mm | 5.1 mm |

**Open-loop fit (Plan A diagnostic, `scripts/fit_error.py`).** Method:
- Put the sim in a recorded state.
- Predict a fresh chunk.
- Compare the predicted TCP targets with the demo's executed ones, for the arm that moves, in mm.

60k checkpoint:

| frames | training takes, median (p90) | unseen takes, median (p90) |
|---|---|---|
| 0.6 s before a grasp | 7.2 (11.5) | 7.8 (14.6) |
| 0.6 s before the click | 4.4 (7.8) | 4.6 (10.8) |
| elsewhere | 6.3 | 7.0 |

**Baselines** on the grasp frames:
- "Repeat the previous target": 1.2 mm.
- "Hold the current joints": 5.3 mm.

So the model is worse than holding still at the grasp.

**Error decomposition:**
- 8 samples per frame differ by only ~3 mm; the rest of the error is systematic.
- About half is timing: the predicted path matches the demo path a few steps earlier or later.
- The purely spatial error, the distance to the demo path, is 4.8 mm (p90 9.2) before a grasp and 2.6 mm before the
  click.

**Reading at the time:**
- **Underfitting:** training and unseen errors are equal.
- **Plus compounding:** the 5 mm in open loop grows to 12–20 mm in closed loop.

### 3.8 mix_v5: fine-tune on grasp-offset generated takes (2026-11-07): **level 1 14%** (no gain)

**Setup:**
- Fine-tune from the 60k checkpoint, 30k steps.
- Data: the 50 retimed takes + 116 generated takes whose bricks start shifted between the finger pads, i.e.
  deliberately off-centre grasps. 166 takes, 46 min.

**Result:** base in area 76%, level 1 14%. With 8 steps per chunk: 58% / 8%.

**Failures and grasps:**
- Never lifted: 20 (before: 13).
- Lifted but never put on red: 13 (before: 22).
- On red but not clicked: 10.
- Grasps got **worse**: red 19.0 mm and blue 16.4 mm off centre at lift-off.

**Reading:**
- The policy copied the wider grasp spread instead of learning to place with an off-centre brick.
- Off-centre grasps as *start conditions* add imprecision. They teach no correction.

### 3.9 Expert E1: Diffusion on 200 scripted-expert demos (2026-11-11/12): **level 1 2%**

**Question:** if the data is perfectly consistent and plentiful, does the same model learn the task? Is the
bottleneck the human data or the model?

**The expert** (`experiments/expert_dagger/expert.py`) is a privileged scripted expert.
- It knows the true brick poses. It runs per-arm Python generators through the same IK → safety filter path as the
  operator.
- Motions are min-jerk.
- **Left arm:** picks red and places it in the build area, yaw 90° ±10° (the way the human operator does), position
  jitter ±12 mm. Then it releases and leaves.
- **Right arm:** picks blue and waits at a staging point 21 cm from the zone (±2 cm jitter) until the left arm has
  left. Then it places blue:
  1. align 25 mm above, using the live in-hand transform;
  2. descend to 7 mm above the studs and settle;
  3. descend straight at 12 mm/s with an xy correction integral;
  4. press up to 6 mm below the seat for ≤ 1.5 s until the click;
  5. if there is no click, lift and retry, up to 3 tries.
- Speed is randomized 0.18–0.28 m/s, with a start delay of 0–0.4 s.
- It succeeds in **100%** of cases.
- **Data:** 200 takes, all clicked, no contacts, click at 16.2 s (median; p10 14.9, p90 17.9). 56.5 min, 169k frames.
  Pipeline check 17/17.

**Result:** run `20261111-1316_dp_240_pre_expert_v2`, `dp_240_pre`, 80k steps. The final loss was 0.001 (human v4:
0.002).

| | base in area | level 1 |
|---|---|---|
| expert | 100% | 100% |
| policy, 16 steps per chunk | 76% | **2%** |
| policy, 8 steps per chunk | 62% | 0% |

**Diagnostics:**
- **Open-loop fit on its own training frames** (8 episodes): grasp 3.8 mm (p90 9), **place 1.4 mm (p90 2.2)**,
  elsewhere 2.6 mm. This is ~3× more precise than the human-data model. The model fits the expert to the millimetre.
- **Timing is fine:** the policy reaches the red brick at 16.1 s (median), the expert at 15.0 s. Only 2 of 50
  rollouts arrive after 24 s; the horizon is 28 s.
- **Closed-loop failure:** 41 of 50 rollouts bring blue onto red. In red's frame, blue lands **20–70 mm short along
  red's long axis**, on the side the right arm approaches from. The goal is −8 mm in red's frame, 19 mm high when
  seated. The policy's blue sits at dz 20–25 mm (on the stud tops or overhanging), the gripper stays closed, and the
  time runs out.
- **Timelines:** the arm already descends while still 3–6 cm short, cutting the corner. The expert first aligns at
  hover height, then descends. Once blue touches the studs it cannot slide any more.
- In one rollout the right arm waited at the staging point until 24 s (the left arm was long done), then placed
  correctly in xy (2 mm) but ran out of time while pressing.
- The red base reaches the area in only 76%, versus 100% for the expert.

**Reading:** this is **not** a capacity or underfitting problem. The model reproduces the expert's frames to
1–2 mm. The cause is **closed-loop compounding (covariate shift)**:
- The expert's takes are nearly identical.
- The data contains no state "a few cm off, not yet aligned" and no correction from there.
- The human takes are messier and contain corrections, which probably explains why human data did better
  (14–16%) than perfect expert data (2%).

Across all runs, **no policy has ever reliably placed a brick within the ~3 mm needed to seat it**. Two failure
modes recur:
1. **Imprecise or off-centre grasps** that get worse in closed loop.
2. **Placement off by 7 mm to several cm, with no retry or correction behaviour.**

## 5. What we currently believe (interpretation, open to challenge)

1. **The pipeline is correct.** Rendering, labels, configs, replay and eval/train consistency are all checked by
   automated tests, and every recent dataset passed.
2. **The model can fit mm precision when the data is consistent** (expert: 1.4 mm open loop). It could not on the
   human data (4–7 mm), so human demo variability contributes to the underfitting.
3. **Closed-loop compounding is the dominant failure.**
   - The expert result is the cleanest evidence: a near-perfect open-loop fit, yet a several-cm closed-loop error.
   - Nothing in any dataset shows recovery from the states the policy actually visits: misaligned above the tower,
     brick on the stud tops, off-centre grasp. The human data has a few such states, and it scores better.
4. **Perception matters, but less.** The state oracle got only to 22%.
5. **Things that did not help:**
   - end-effector instead of joint actions;
   - more denoising steps;
   - checkpoint averaging;
   - a bigger stud lead-in on its own;
   - longer training past 60k;
   - grasp-offset start conditions;
   - perfectly consistent expert data.
6. **Recent changes that did help** a little: the pretrained encoder, the `cam_low` side view, and narrowing the task
   to the first two bricks.

## 6. Candidate next steps we have considered (not yet done)

- **Expert v3 with DART-style perturbations.**
  - Inject noise into the expert's executed targets and approach waypoints, and label with its clean targets, so
    every take contains corrections from off-nominal states.
  - CPU only, about one night.
- **Automatic DAgger.**
  - The policy drives; the privileged expert labels every visited state, or takes over near failure. Aggregate the
    data and retrain.
  - Needs the expert to react to any state, not just follow its phase programme.
- **Human DAgger.** Shared control is implemented: the policy drives and the operator takes over with the Grip
  button. Only the operator's frames are used for training. Not recorded yet.
- **Relative actions:** targets relative to the current joints instead of absolute, as UMI and π0 do. The aim is
  better mm resolution after normalization.
- **EMA** of the weights (the wrapper exists, untested in a full run).
- **Higher-resolution wrist crops**, or 2 observation frames.
- **Residual RL** on a frozen diffusion policy (Ankile et al. 2024), with the click as the reward.
- **Hybrid control:** the policy brings the brick within ~1 cm, and a classical force-controlled seating primitive
  (press + small spiral search) finishes the job.
- **A realistic stud lead-in:** a larger chamfer or alignment tolerance. This changes the task and invalidates
  the data.
- **Normalization of `environment_state`** in the oracle (suspected bug in the LeRobot setup).

## 7. Constraints any proposal must respect

- **Hardware:** RTX 4050 6 GB (Diffusion with 3 cameras at 240×320 uses ~5.1 GB at batch 32) and 14 GB RAM (at most
  ~3 parallel simulation workers). A full training run takes ~9.5 h; a 50-rollout eval ~15 min.
- **Simulation first, but real robots will follow.** Policy inputs should be things a real Franka setup also has:
  cameras, joints, the estimated wrench. Privileged state is fine for experts and labelling only.
- **The user prefers realistic data:** human demos, natural mistakes, DART, DAgger. No assists that make data
  unrealistically clean. Scripted and generated data is acceptable as an experiment.
- **The two-brick work is a feasibility test.** It must stay isolated (`experiments/`, its own configs and data
  dirs), so the full 4-brick setup at the repo root stays intact. Do not change the scene, physics or root defaults
  for it.
- LeRobot 0.4.4 is the training framework (Diffusion, ACT available). VLAs (π0, GR00T, SmolVLA) do not fit 6 GB.

## 9. Questions for the reader

1. Given the expert result (1.4 mm open loop, 2–7 cm closed loop), is covariate shift really the main cause? Or does
   something in the closed-loop setup systematically shift the policy, for example the safety filter, the
   chunk-boundary execution or n_obs_steps = 1?
2. Which is most likely to get level 1 above ~50% within a few nights on this hardware: perturbed expert data,
   automatic DAgger, relative actions, or a hybrid seating primitive?
3. Why does the human-data model's open-loop error (4–7 mm) not fall with more training (flat after 60k), while the
   expert-data model reaches 1.4 mm?
4. Is a 50 Hz absolute-joint action with min-max normalization a sound choice for mm-precise insertion, given that
   1 mm at the TCP is ~0.001 rad?
